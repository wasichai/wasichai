[[user-access]]
== Accessing the application

=== Requirements

* A current web browser (the interface uses React 19 and modern browser features; the screenshots in this manual
  were taken with Chromium). Nothing needs to be installed on the computer.
* The address (URL) of the application, which your organization gives you.
* An account: an email and a password created by an administrator (chapter _Users, roles and permissions_ of the
  _Administrator manual_). There is no self-registration and no password recovery from the sign-in screen: if you
  forget your password, ask an administrator to assign you a new one.

[[user-sign-in]]
=== Signing in

.Objective
Enter the application with your account.

.Prerequisites
An active account in the organization.

.Procedure
. Open the address of the application. If you have not signed in, the application takes you to the sign-in screen
  (<<user-fig-login>>).
. Type your email in *Email* and your password in *Password*.
. Click *Sign in*.

[[user-fig-login]]
.Sign-in screen
image::screenshots/{lang}/user/01-login.png[Sign-in card with the name of the application, the Email and Password fields and the Sign in button,pdfwidth=50%]

.Expected result
The *Home* page opens (<<user-fig-home>>). Your name and your email appear at the foot of the side menu.

.Verification
Your name at the foot of the menu confirms which account you signed in with.

.Special cases
* If the email or the password is not correct, the card shows the message *Invalid email or password*
  (<<user-fig-login-error>>). The server sends this message and writes it in English, whatever the interface
  language. For security, the message is the same whether the email does not exist or the password is wrong.
* An account disabled by an administrator cannot sign in and gets the same message.
* If the organization turned on the attempt limit, after several failed attempts in a row sign-in is blocked for a
  while, even with the right password (<<user-messages>>).

[[user-session]]
=== Session length

When you sign in, the server gives your browser a temporary credential (a _token_). While it is valid, the
application does not ask for your password again, even if you close the tab. By default it lasts 8 hours; your
organization may have configured another length.

When the credential expires, or when an administrator disables your account or changes your password, the next
action you take gets a "not authenticated" response and the application sends you back to the sign-in screen.
Whatever you were typing and had not saved is lost.

TIP: If you are going to leave a long form half done, save it first: the application does not keep drafts.

[[user-sign-out]]
=== Signing out

.Objective
Leave the application so that nobody else uses your session on that computer.

.Procedure
. Click *Sign out*, at the foot of the side menu.

.Expected result
The application deletes the credential from the browser and shows the sign-in screen.

.Special cases
NOTE: If your organization turned on credential revocation, when you sign out the server also invalidates every
credential issued to you up to that moment, including those of other browsers. Without that option, a credential
copied out of the browser would stay valid until it expires. Always sign out on shared computers.
