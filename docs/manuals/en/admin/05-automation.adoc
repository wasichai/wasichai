[[admin-automation]]
== Workflows and automation

[[admin-workflows]]
=== Workflows

An object can have a workflow: states and transitions. menu:Automation[Workflows] draws them on a canvas
(<<admin-fig-workflow>>).

:admin-workflow-alt: Canvas with the states Borrador, En revisión, Observada, Aprobada and Rechazada joined by \
transitions, and the Details panel
[[admin-fig-workflow]]
.Canvas of the workflow "Trámite de licencia"
image::screenshots/{lang}/admin/15-workflow-canvas.png["{admin-workflow-alt}",pdfwidth=100%]

.Objective
Define the life cycle of the records of an object.

.Procedure
. Pick the *Object*. If it has no workflow, the screen offers a starter template (draft → review →
  approved/rejected).
. *Add state* creates a state; in the *Details* panel set its technical name, its label and its type: *Initial*
  (exactly one), *Intermediate* or *Final*.
. To create a transition, drag from the dot of one state to another. Select it and fill in name, label and the
  *roles* that can apply it (empty: anyone with `UPDATE`).
. Select the empty canvas to edit the technical name and the label of the workflow, and the *Enabled* checkbox.
. *Rearrange* lays out the drawing automatically. Click *Save workflow*.

.Expected result
The detail page of the records shows the state and the transition buttons.

.Special cases
* A disabled workflow shows no buttons on the record.
* New records start in the initial state. Those that already existed when the workflow was added have no stored
  state and are treated as if they were in the initial one: they show its transitions.
* Each transition stays in the audit log and can fire rules (_A record enters a state_, _A transition is
  applied_).

WARNING: *Delete workflow* deletes the definition. The records keep in the database the last state they had, but
no transitions can be applied until the object has a new workflow.

[[admin-rules]]
=== Automation rules

A rule says: _when_ something happens to a record, _if_ some conditions are met, _do_ some actions.
menu:Automation[Rules] (<<admin-fig-rules>>).

:admin-rules-alt: Editor of a rule: technical name, label, Enabled checkbox, trigger A record enters a state Aprobada, \
no conditions, and the action Issue a document
[[admin-fig-rules]]
.The rule "Emitir certificado al aprobar"
image::screenshots/{lang}/admin/16-automation-rules.png["{admin-rules-alt}",pdfwidth=100%]

Triggers (*When*)::
When a record is created, updated or deleted; when a transition is applied (optionally a specific one); when a
specific state is entered.
Conditions::
On a field or on the state: equals, does not equal, greater than, less than, contains, empty, not empty, changed.
With no conditions, the rule acts every time the trigger happens.
Actions::
* *Write a field* of the record.
* *Create a record* in another object.
* *Call a webhook*: an HTTP call to a public URL (private addresses are not accepted).
* *Issue a document* of a type of the object.
* *Notify* people: user ids or emails, `role:NOMBRE` or `unit:CÓDIGO` (requires the notifications module).

In the texts the record can be read with double braces: `{{codigo}}`, and also `{{id}}`, `{{state}}`, `{{user}}`,
`{{today}}` and `{{now}}`.

.Procedure
. Pick the *Object*, click *New rule* and fill in *Technical name*, *Label* and *Enabled*.
. Configure *When*, *Conditions* (*Add condition*) and *Actions* (*Add action*).
. Click *Save rule*.

.Special cases
* The actions run in the background, shortly after the change (by default, the process checks the queue every
  second), and act as the platform, not as the person: in the history they appear as *System* with the reason
  `automation '<nombre>'`.
* A rule can fire others; the chain is cut at 3 levels of depth.
* A field used by a rule cannot be deleted until it is removed from the rule.

[[admin-runs]]
=== Runs

menu:Automation[Runs] shows what fired, what did not and why (<<admin-fig-runs>>). It can be filtered by *Status*
and by amount.

[[admin-fig-runs]]
.Runs of the rules of the example
image::screenshots/{lang}/admin/17-automation-runs.png["Table of runs with status Done, rule, trigger, date and the detail of each action",pdfwidth=100%]

Each row shows the status (done, skipped with its reason, failed), the rule, the trigger, when it happened and the
detail of each action. Reading the runs only requires `READ` on the object, so that whoever saw a record change can
find out why.

NOTE: In version {ui-version} of the interface, the detail of the _Notify_ action appears with the untranslated key
`automations.actionTypes.NOTIFY`. It is a defect in the texts of the interface, not a run error.
