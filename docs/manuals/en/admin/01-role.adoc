[[admin-role]]
== Functions and responsibilities

=== Who is an administrator

There is no single "administrator": there are permissions that enable each task, and the special role `ADMIN`, which
gathers them.

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
|Permission or role |Enables
|`ADMIN` |Everything inside the organization. It skips the permission checks (not the _append-only_ or _application
only_ rules). It cannot be deleted. It never applies to a service account.
|`MANAGE_METADATA` |Objects, fields, relationships, declared actions, views, forms, pages, document types, workflows
and automation rules.
|`MANAGE_ORGANIZATION` |Users, roles, permissions, service accounts, organizational units, notifications, the name
of the organization and reading the administration entries of the audit log.
|`MANAGE_TENANTS` |Create and delete organizations (tenants). By default whoever has `MANAGE_ORGANIZATION` has it;
with `wasichai.organizations.separate-provisioning=true`, only an explicit grant.
|===

`MANAGE_METADATA` and `MANAGE_ORGANIZATION` are granted "for every object" (the *Every object* row of the
permission matrix, <<admin-permissions>>).

=== First access to an environment

The platform creates no administrator in production. There are two ways:

* *Development or demo environment*: with `wasichai.seed.dev=true`, the first migration creates the organization
  _Demo_ (`demo`), the role `ADMIN` and the user `admin@wasichai.local` with password `admin`. Development only.
* *Production*: an operator who already has `MANAGE_TENANTS` creates the organization and its first administrator
  with `POST /api/organizations` (<<admin-tenants>>). The first holder of that permission is granted outside the
  application, as the guide _Build your app_ in the repository describes.

WARNING: Do not turn on `wasichai.seed.dev` in production: it creates an account with a publicly known password.

=== Usual responsibilities

* Keep the data model up to date with the work of the organization.
* Add and remove people, and review their roles periodically.
* Grant each role only what it needs (least privilege).
* Review the audit log when incidents happen.
* Coordinate backups, upgrades and restores with the technical team.
